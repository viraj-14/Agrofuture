What is Agriculture?

The Science &
Art of Farming

Agriculture is the practice of cultivating plants, raising livestock and other practices for food, fiber, medicine and other products used to sustain and enhance life. Modern agriculture uses scientific methods and technology to improve efficiency.

🌟 Did You Know?

India has 140.1 million hectares of arable land — 2nd largest in the world — yet yields remain below global averages due to outdated methods. Smart agriculture can change this.

Step by Step

The Farming Process

A complete journey from planning to harvest, simplified for every farmer.

Step 01
Land Selection & Preparation
Choose the right soil type, test pH levels, and prepare the land through plowing, leveling and bed formation. Ideal pH for most crops is 6.0–7.5.
Step 02
Seed Selection & Sowing
Select certified, high-yield seeds suitable for your region and season. Use seed treatment to protect against early disease. Sow at the right depth and spacing.
Step 03
Irrigation & Water Management
Apply water at critical growth stages. Drip irrigation reduces water consumption by 40–50% compared to flood irrigation. Monitor soil moisture regularly.
Step 04
Nutrient & Fertilizer Management
Follow soil test recommendations. Apply NPK fertilizers in splits — basal dose at sowing and top dressing at active growth stages. Integrate organic matter.
Step 05
Pest & Disease Control
Follow IPM — Integrated Pest Management. Monitor regularly, use bio-control agents first, then targeted chemical applications only when thresholds are crossed.
Step 06
Harvesting & Post-Harvest
Harvest at optimal maturity. Proper drying, cleaning, grading and storage in ventilated structures reduces post-harvest losses from 30% to below 5%.

FAQ

Common Farming Questions

Answers to the questions every beginner farmer asks.

For beginners, start with easy-to-grow crops like tomatoes, leafy vegetables (spinach, coriander), beans, and radish. These have short growing seasons, high demand, and are forgiving of minor mistakes.
Watering frequency depends on the crop, soil type and climate. A general rule: check if the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Most vegetables need watering every 2–3 days. Drip irrigation systems automate this perfectly.
Healthy soil is dark, loose, and holds moisture without becoming waterlogged. It smells earthy (petrichor), has earthworms, and crumbles easily. Get a soil test from your KVK or agriculture department for accurate results.
India has two main farming seasons: Kharif (June–November, monsoon crops like rice, maize, cotton) and Rabi (October–March, winter crops like wheat, mustard, gram). Start planning 4–6 weeks before the season begins.
